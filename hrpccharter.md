# Human Rights and Policy Considerations Research Group

The Human Rights and Policy Considerations (HRPC) research group explores the relationship between internet architecture and
human rights. The role of the research group is to promote
understanding of this relationship so those making decisions can do so in an
informed manner. It aims to inform the technical community
of how the design of protocols impacts, and can be informed by, policies
that serve the public interest and the protection of human rights. It aims to make policy makers and civil society groups aware of the technical constraints that might impact policy choices.

## Background

The research group takes as its starting point the statement that
human-rights-enabling characteristics of the internet might be degraded if
they are not properly defined, described, and sufficiently taken into
account in protocol and standards development.

It is widely accepted that technical design decisions about the
internet are not value neutral and can have lasting impacts on public
policy and individual human rights. Studying those impacts is the main
objective of this group.

The internet is a global network of networks that aims to provide
unfettered connectivity to all users at all times and for any content.
Open, secure, and reliable connectivity is essential for rights such as
freedom of expression and freedom of association. Since the internet's
objective of connectivity intersects with human rights, its architectural
design considerations overlap with the human rights framework. This
framework is rooted in the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR)
[1], and encompasses both civil and political rights, as set out in the
International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR) [2], and
economic, social and cultural rights, as set out in the International
Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (ICESCR) [3].

Our main research question is: "How are human rights and public interest
policy (as a cross section of public policy focused on human rights)
considered during the development of the internet?" HRPC researches
whether and how internet standards and protocols enable, strengthen, or
threaten human rights. It also studies how protocols shape, and are
shaped by, human-rights-related public policy.

The group analyses applications and services where their behaviour
illuminates consequences of internet protocols, architectures, or
deployment practices. Contributions should distinguish technical design
choices from implementation decisions, service policies, and institutional
practices, and make clear where potential interventions lie.

## Objectives

 * To expose the relations between protocols, policy and human rights values.
   Contributions should identify a specific internet protocol, architectural
   mechanism, or deployment practice; explain how it contributes to the
   consequences under investigation; and identify findings that can inform
   technical design or operation.

 * To suggest guidelines based on findings to protect the internet as a human-rights-enabling
   environment and a global public good in future protocol development.

 * To increase awareness in both the human rights community and the
   technical community of the importance of the internet's technical
   workings, and their impact on human rights and the public interest and to promote our findings.

 * To create a place for discussions and analysis of the relationship
   between protocol development, and their human rights and policy
   implications by, among other mechanisms, serving as a bridge between
   the human rights and the protocol's development communities.

Note: HRPC does not set policy for the IETF nor speak on behalf of the IETF or IRTF.
